Choosing a Warm Jacket

The warmest jacket is the one that suits the weather you actually face. Here is how to choose.

01

Start with your weather

Dry, still cold suits down. Damp, wet cold suits synthetic insulation or a down jacket with a waterproof shell. Windy days need a windproof outer layer whatever is inside. Think about where and how you will wear it most.

02

Down and fill power

Fill power measures how much space an ounce of down fills. Higher numbers (700 and above) mean more warmth for less weight and a smaller pack size. Fill weight, the total amount of down, decides how warm the jacket is overall.

03

Synthetic insulation

Synthetic fills mimic down’s trapped air with fine fibres. They keep insulating when damp, dry quickly and are easier to wash, at the cost of a little more weight and bulk. They suit wet climates and active days.

04

Features that matter

  • An insulated hood that fits over a hat.
  • Draught flaps behind the zip and snug cuffs.
  • A length that covers the lower back.
  • Hand-warmer pockets lined with fleece.
05

Fit for layering

Try jackets on over the layers you will wear. You should be able to raise your arms without the hem lifting far and fasten the zip without compressing the insulation, which needs loft to keep you warm.
