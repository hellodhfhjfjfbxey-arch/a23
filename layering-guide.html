Layering for the Cold

Three layers, each with one job, beat one heavy coat. Add and remove them as the weather and your effort change.

01

The base layer

Worn next to the skin, the base layer moves sweat away so you stay dry. Choose merino wool or a synthetic fabric. Avoid cotton in the cold: it holds moisture and chills you quickly.

02

The mid layer

The mid layer traps warm air. Fleece is breathable and quick-drying; wool is warm and comfortable; a light insulated jacket is the warmest for its weight. Wear more than one mid layer on very cold days.

03

The outer shell

The shell blocks wind and rain. A waterproof breathable jacket protects everything underneath. On dry, cold days, a windproof softshell or an insulated jacket can act as the outer layer.

04

Adjust as you go

  • Start a walk slightly cool; you will warm up within minutes.
  • Open zips before you sweat, not after.
  • Add the warm layer as soon as you stop.
  • Keep hands, head and neck covered: they lose heat fast.
05

City layering

The same system works in town: a thin merino or thermal top, a knit, and a warm coat. You stay comfortable on cold streets and can take a layer off on a warm train or in a shop.
